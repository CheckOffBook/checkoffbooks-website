How Much Do Monthly Bookkeeping Services Cost for a Small Business?

Bookkeeping prices vary widely. Here is what actually drives the bill, how the common pricing models work, and how to compare your options.

Why bookkeeping prices vary so much

Ask five bookkeepers for a quote and you can get five very different numbers. That is not because some are honest and some are not. It is because "bookkeeping" covers a wide range of work, from categorizing a few dozen transactions a month to managing payroll, bills, invoicing, and inventory for a growing company.

Before you compare prices, it helps to know what you are actually buying and which parts of the work drive the cost.

The common pricing models

Hourly billing

Some bookkeepers bill by the hour. It can work well for irregular or one-time projects, but your monthly bill can swing up and down with your transaction volume, questions, and cleanup needs. It is also hard to know the cost in advance.

Monthly flat fee

Many services charge a set monthly price for a defined scope, often tied to the number of accounts or the volume of transactions. A flat fee makes budgeting simple, as long as you understand exactly what is included and what costs extra.

Software plus a bookkeeper

Another common setup is paying for accounting software and then paying someone to work inside it, or doing the work yourself. Owners in this situation are often paying twice: once for the software subscription and again in time spent fixing bank feeds, re-categorizing transactions, and chasing reconciliations.

What drives your monthly bookkeeping bill

  • Number of accounts. Every checking account, savings account, and credit card is another set of transactions to categorize and reconcile.
  • Transaction volume. A business with hundreds of transactions a month takes more work than one with a few dozen.
  • Catch-up work. If the books are months or years behind, expect a separate cleanup cost on top of the ongoing monthly price.
  • Add-on services. Payroll, bill pay, invoicing, sales tax filings, and inventory tracking usually cost extra or require a different kind of provider.
  • Reporting needs. A monthly Profit & Loss is standard. A Balance Sheet, accrual-basis reporting, or custom reports may require more information and more work.

What an affordable bookkeeping service should still include

Affordable should not mean incomplete. At a minimum, a monthly bookkeeping service should:

  • Categorize every transaction on the accounts you include
  • Reconcile those accounts to the statements
  • Deliver a clear Profit & Loss you can review and ask questions about
  • Tell you up front what the price covers, what costs extra, and how to cancel

Be cautious about any service that is vague about scope. A low headline price that turns into add-on fees is not affordable.

Questions to ask before you sign up

  • How many accounts does the monthly price cover, and what does each extra account cost?
  • Do I have to link my bank accounts or learn new software?
  • What reports will I get, and when?
  • Does the price ever go up?
  • Can I cancel anytime?
  • Do you prepare tax returns or give tax advice? (Many bookkeeping services do not, so plan to work with a tax professional separately.)
