Bookkeeping From Bank Statements: A Simpler Way to Keep Small Business Books

Tired of broken bank feeds and doing your own bookkeeping inside software? There is another way to keep clean books.

The problem with bank-feed bookkeeping

Most accounting software is built around bank feeds: you link your bank and credit-card accounts, and transactions flow in automatically. In theory, that saves time. In practice, many small business owners run into the same problems:

  • Connections break and need to be re-authorized
  • Transactions show up twice, or not at all
  • Automatic rules miscategorize transactions, and someone has to fix them
  • You end up paying for software and still spending hours doing the bookkeeping yourself

Some owners also simply do not want to hand their bank login to a third-party app.

How statement-based bookkeeping works

Statement-based bookkeeping skips the feed. Instead, the books are built from the official statements your bank and card issuers already produce every month.

  1. Download. Each month, download your statements from your bank's website as PDF or CSV files.
  2. Upload. Send them to your bookkeeper through a secure portal.
  3. Categorize and reconcile. The bookkeeper categorizes every transaction and reconciles each account to the statement.
  4. Review. You receive a cash-basis Profit & Loss to review, with any questions about unclear transactions.

Because the statement is the source, there is nothing to sync and nothing to break.

Who it fits

  • Small businesses that want clean monthly books without learning software
  • Contractors and consultants with straightforward business accounts
  • Owners who do not want to link bank logins to third-party apps
  • Businesses that are behind on their books and need to rebuild past months from statements

Its limits

Statement-based bookkeeping works from what is on the statements. That means:

  • Some transactions need context the statement cannot provide, such as what a check was for or whether a transfer was an owner contribution. Your bookkeeper will ask, and supporting documents may be needed.
  • It produces cash-basis reports. If you need accrual-basis accounting, inventory tracking, or invoicing, you will need other tools for those pieces.
  • A Balance Sheet requires additional information about assets, loans, and equity beyond what bank statements show.

For many small businesses, those limits do not matter. For others, they are a reason to choose a different setup. Either way, it is better to know up front.
