What Small Business Bookkeeping Services Include (and What They Don't)

Know what you are paying for. Here is what bookkeeping covers, what it usually does not, and how to pick the right setup for your business.

What bookkeeping is

Bookkeeping is the ongoing work of recording and organizing your business's financial transactions so you have accurate numbers. It is the foundation that tax preparation, financial planning, and lending decisions are built on, but it is not the same as any of those.

What bookkeeping services typically include

  • Transaction categorization. Every deposit and expense is assigned to the right income or expense category.
  • Account reconciliation. Your books are matched against your bank and credit-card statements so the balances agree and nothing is missing or duplicated.
  • Monthly financial reports. Most commonly a Profit & Loss (income statement) showing revenue, expenses, and net income for the month and year to date.
  • Balance Sheet (sometimes). Some services include it; others provide it on request because it requires additional information about assets, loans, and equity.
  • Questions and clarifications. A good bookkeeper will ask about transactions they cannot identify rather than guess.

What bookkeeping services usually do not include

  • Tax return preparation. Preparing and filing returns is typically done by a separate tax professional.
  • Tax advice or planning. Bookkeepers organize the numbers; decisions about tax strategy belong with a qualified tax advisor.
  • Payroll. Running payroll is often a separate service or software.
  • Invoicing, bill pay, and collections. Some full-service firms do this, but many bookkeeping services do not.
  • Legal or CFO-level advice. Business structure, contracts, and strategic finance are outside a bookkeeper's role.

Knowing these boundaries up front prevents surprises and helps you compare services fairly.

Three ways to get your books done

Do it yourself with software

Accounting software can work if you enjoy the process and have the time. The trade-off is that you are still the bookkeeper: fixing broken bank connections, reviewing feeds, and categorizing transactions every month.

Hire a bookkeeper

A part-time or freelance bookkeeper takes the work off your plate. Costs and processes vary widely, and many will still expect you to use, and pay for, accounting software.

Use a done-for-you service

A done-for-you service handles the work end to end for a defined price. The key questions are what you need to provide, what you get back, and what the price covers.
