Year-End Bookkeeping Checklist for Small Businesses

Close out the year with clean books. Work through this checklist before you hand anything to your tax preparer.

Why year-end bookkeeping matters

Your tax preparer can only work with the records you give them. If the year's transactions are uncategorized, unreconciled, or mixed with personal spending, someone has to sort it out before the return can be prepared, and that usually means more time, more questions, and more cost.

A clean set of books at year-end makes that handoff simpler. Use the checklist below to get there.

1. Gather every statement for the year

Download statements for every business account for all twelve months, including December once it closes:

  • Business checking and savings accounts
  • Business credit cards
  • Payment processors and platforms that deposit money to your bank, if they hold activity your bank statement does not show in detail

PDF or CSV statements from your bank's website are usually all you need. Check that no month is missing for any account.

2. Reconcile each account

For every account, confirm that the transactions in your books match the statement and that the ending balance agrees. Reconciling catches duplicate entries, missing transactions, and errors that would otherwise flow straight into your year-end numbers.

3. Separate personal and business transactions

If personal purchases ran through a business account, or business expenses ran through a personal card, flag them. Your tax preparer will need to know which is which, and clean separation going forward makes next year easier.

4. Clear out uncategorized and miscategorized items

Review anything sitting in "uncategorized," "ask my accountant," or a catch-all category. Look for recurring vendors that were categorized inconsistently during the year and fix them so your expense categories tell an accurate story.

5. Pull together vendor information

Make a list of the contractors and vendors you paid during the year, along with their contact information. Your tax preparer may need this to determine whether any information returns, such as Form 1099, apply. Ask them what they need rather than guessing.

6. Review what is owed to you and what you owe

List unpaid customer invoices and unpaid bills at year-end. Even if you keep cash-basis books, this is useful context for your tax preparer and for planning the year ahead.

7. Note big purchases, loans, and owner transactions

Equipment and vehicle purchases, loan proceeds and payments, and money you put into or took out of the business are easy to misclassify as ordinary income or expenses. Flag them so they are handled correctly.

8. Produce a clean cash-basis Profit & Loss

Once everything is categorized and reconciled, run a Profit & Loss for the full year with month-by-month detail. This is the summary your tax preparer will start from, and it is the clearest picture you have of how the business actually did.

9. Hand off and ask questions

Send your tax preparer the Profit & Loss, the statements, your vendor list, and your notes. Tax questions, such as what is deductible or how a specific transaction should be treated, belong with them.
