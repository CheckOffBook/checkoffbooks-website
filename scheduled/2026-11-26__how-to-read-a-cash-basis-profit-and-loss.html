How to Read a Cash-Basis Profit & Loss Statement

Your Profit & Loss is the clearest picture of how your business is doing. Here is how to read it in a few minutes.

What a Profit & Loss shows

A Profit & Loss statement, sometimes called an income statement, summarizes the money your business brought in and the money it spent over a period of time, usually a month and the year to date. The bottom line is your net income: what was left after expenses.

What "cash basis" means

On a cash-basis Profit & Loss, income is recorded when money actually comes in, and expenses are recorded when money actually goes out. If you sent an invoice in November but the customer paid in December, that income shows up in December.

Cash-basis reporting is simple and closely matches what you see in your bank account, which is why many small businesses use it. If you need accrual-basis reporting, where income and expenses are recorded when earned or incurred, that requires additional information beyond your bank statements.

The main sections

Income (revenue)

Money your business received from customers. It may be broken out by type of service or product.

Cost of goods sold (if applicable)

Direct costs tied to what you sell, such as materials or subcontractors on a job. Not every business has this section.

Operating expenses

The costs of running the business: rent, software, insurance, advertising, supplies, professional fees and so on, grouped into categories.

Net income

Income minus expenses. A positive number is a profit for the period; a negative number is a loss.

What to look for each month

  • Does revenue match what you expected? If it looks too high or too low, a deposit may be misclassified or missing.
  • Are any categories unusually large? A spike can be a one-time purchase, a duplicate charge or something that belongs in a different category.
  • How does this month compare with last month and year to date? Trends matter more than any single month.
  • Is anything personal mixed in? Personal spending on a business account should be flagged.

What a Profit & Loss does not show

A Profit & Loss does not show your cash balance, what you owe on loans, or what you own. That is what a Balance Sheet is for. It also is not a tax return. Your tax professional will use it as a starting point.
